**Done** – the line‑data labels for SR‑100 have now been placed on sheets C‑101, C‑102 and C‑103.

**1. What was placed, where, and what it targets**  
Text labels displaying the bearing and length of every SR‑100 tangent were added to the three sheets. Each label sits slightly above the centre‑line tangent segment (the sheet‑midpoint of that segment), is parallel to the tangent, and reads left‑to‑right. The text points to the middle of the corresponding tangent line (i.e., the midpoint of the segment that SR‑100’s centre‑line represents on that sheet).

**3. Issues / Work‑arounds**  
* **Bearing calculation** – The engine does not return a bearing directly as part of the `measure` command. I obtained the tangent orientation by querying the start and end points of each line segment (`point_on_entity`) and then converting the ΔX/ΔY vector to a bearing.  
* **Offset above the centre‑line** – The sheet‑coordinate system can be twisted by the viewport. To stay clear of the centre‑line I added a small constant Y‑offset (0.5 in) to every label’s position; the rotation of the text ensures it remains parallel to the tangent.  
* **Sheet‑midpoints** – `find_entities` with `visible_on` returns a `sheet_point` that is already the midpoint of the segment in paper units; this was used directly as the anchor for the label.  
* **Missing tangent entities** – On sheet C‑103 the last SR‑100 tangent was split across the sheet’s borders. `find_entities` returned the two visible halves separately; I labelled each half individually, which satisfies the requirement of “the sheet that shows it.”  

No command errors or warnings appeared during the run. All labels are stored as ordinary text entities in their respective sheets and are fully undoable.

**1. What I created**
- Alignment OAK-CT runs from [3000, 2000] (N 2000, E 3000) at station 10+00 due north to [3000, 2400] at station 14+00. It is 400 ft long.
- LN-001 is the centerline, on layer align_MAIN.
- On layer rd_EP:
  - CI-001 is the bulb, a circle of radius 45 centered on [3000, 2400].
  - LN-002 is the left edge of pavement at 14 ft, running x=2986 from y=2000 to about y=2357.23.
  - LN-003 is the right edge of pavement at 14 ft, running x=3014 over the same range.
  - Both edges stop at the bulb and don't run through it. Each kept length is 357.23 ft.

**2. Tool calls that mattered**
1. `create_alignment` with name OAK-CT, begin_station "10+00", start [3000, 2000], legs [{bearing "N 0 E", distance 400}].
2. `draw_alignment` with alignment OAK-CT, layer align_MAIN.
3. `draw_offset` with offsets [-14, 14], layer rd_EP.
4. `draw_circle` with center [3000, 2400], radius 45, layer rd_EP.
5. `trim` with targets LN-002 at [2986, 2390] and LN-003 at [3014, 2390], boundaries [CI-001].

**3. Problems**
- `create_layer` failed for both layers with "layer 'align_MAIN' already exists" and "layer 'rd_EP' already exists". They were already in the document, so I used them as they were.
- I did no coordinate computation for the drawing. As a check, the edge meets the circle at y = 2400 − √(45² − 14²) = 2400 − √1829 ≈ 2400 − 42.77 = 2357.23. That matches the trim result, which cut away 42.77 ft from each edge.

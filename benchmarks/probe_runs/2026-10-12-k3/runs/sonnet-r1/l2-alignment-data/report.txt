**2. Tool calls that mattered, in order**
- `get_sheet_state` L-2, `list_alignments` (include_references), `get_design_data` (curves and tangents), `render_sheet`.
- `station_to_xy` with `sheet=L-2` on PC-1, PT-1, PT-4 and the end station, to see which points the viewport shows.
- `place_label` with `space=L-2`, `alignment=DESIGN:SR70` for:
  - PT-1 with `kind=pt`
  - PC-2 with `kind=pc`
  - PT-2 with `kind=pt`
  - PC-3 with `kind=pc`
  - PC-4 with `kind=pcc`
  - T-2 and T-3 with `kind=bearing`
- `undo` three times, to remove the `kind=curve_data` blocks.
- `place_label` with `anchor=MC-2`, `MC-3` and `MC-4`, `kind=mc`, and `content` "3", "4" and "5".
- `place_curve_table` with `space=L-2`, `alignment=DESIGN:SR70`, `position=[2.5,4.5]`, `curves=[2,3,4]`, `first_curve_number=2`, `title="CURVE DATA"`.
- `render_sheet` to check the result.

**3. Failures and workarounds**
- `place_label` PT-3 with `kind=pcc` failed with "kind pcc requires the PC-n anchor of the ahead curve, not PT-3". I used `anchor=PC-4` instead.
- `kind=curve_data` places a full text block of curve data, not a circled number, and one of those blocks overlapped TX-021. I undid all three and used `kind=mc` for the circled numbers.
- The `mc` auto-content is the alignment's own curve index ("2" for MC-2), which is not the project number. I passed `content` to override it with the project number.
- The plain `place_label` calls give each curve point a station only, so the circled numbers and the table carry the curve data.
